What we do

CPCC works exclusively with early-to-mid clinical-stage biotech companies to efficiently navigate the path from candidate selection and confirmation to meaningful clinical inflection points: the data, regulatory decisions, and milestones that unlock program advancement and company growth.

CPCC does both halves of the job. On the operations side, that means someone who can actually run the program: lead the asset, stand up the trials, manage the CROs and vendors, keep budgets and timelines on track, and deliver submission-ready work. On the strategy side, it means a senior thought partner for development planning and for conversations with investors, the board, partners, and regulators.

About

CPCC is led by Amanda McEwen (LinkedIn), a clinical development leader with 16+ years taking novel therapeutics from IND-enabling work to approval, all of it inside small and mid-size biotech. She combines hands-on clinical operations and program leadership with development and regulatory strategy, and that combination is what CPCC brings to client teams.

She was program lead for nedosiran (Rivfloza) at Dicerna, an RNAi therapeutic for ultra-rare primary hyperoxaluria, where she defined the registrational strategy, drove FDA acceptance of a novel surrogate endpoint, and led global development through FDA and EMA approval. Earlier, she led Phase 1–3 operational execution of Inbrija, a drug-device combination for Parkinson's disease, across 13 countries and 72 sites through FDA approval. She has built clinical and regulatory functions from scratch at start-ups, taking Nocion from pre-IND through first-in-human and Phase 2a proof of concept, and at Salubris Biotherapeutics has led JK07, a first-in-class ErbB4 agonist for heart failure, from first-in-human into Phase 2.

Throughout, her focus has been generating the clinical data that companies raise money and do deals on. Nocion's Phase 2a proof-of-concept data supported its Series B, and nedosiran was Dicerna's lead program when Novo Nordisk acquired the company in 2021. She knows what investors, partners, and acquirers look for in a data package, and how to build a program that delivers it.

Because small biotech rarely has clean lines between functions, she embeds easily both with founding teams that need a full clinical function built out and with larger organizations that need senior capacity on a specific workstream or at a critical juncture.

Amanda is an Executive Fellow at Harvard Business School, where she mentors new biotech entrepreneurs, and adjunct faculty in Northeastern University's MS in Regulatory Affairs program, where she teaches therapeutic product development and pharmacovigilance. She is a PhD candidate in Business Analytics at Bentley University, and holds a BA from the College of the Holy Cross and a Master of Research Methods in Psychology from the University of York.

Clinical Data Fuels Fundraising / M&A

Dealmaking is back: 2026 is on pace to be the strongest year for biopharma M&A since the pandemic. But capital still follows clinical evidence. Buyers are reaching earlier, before Phase 3, while IPO and venture investors save their largest checks for companies with clinical validation. For an early-stage company, the quality and timing of its first clinical data package is what turns into value.

$130B Biopharma M&A in the first half of 2026 alone, nearly matching the $133B total for all of 2025. Source: IQVIA
63% Of H1 2026 biopharma deals targeted pre-Phase 3 assets, up from 39% in 2025 and a record for early- and mid-stage acquisitions. Source: EY
12 of 13 US biotech IPOs in H1 2026 were companies with assets in Phase 2 or later. Source: IQVIA
32% Share of biopharma venture funding going to early-stage companies in 2025, down from 44% in 2020, as investors concentrate on later, de-risked programs. Source: PitchBook

Sources: IQVIA, “Biopharma M&A: Outlook for 2026” (Jan 2026) and “Mid-year 2026 update” (Jul 2026), majority-stake acquisitions ≥$250M; EY data as reported by BioXconomy (H1 2026); PitchBook data as reported by Fierce Biotech (2025).
